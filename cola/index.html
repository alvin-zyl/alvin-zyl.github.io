CoLA: Compute-Efficient Pre-Training of LLMs via Low-Rank Activation

In short

The activations inside a pre-trained LLM are low-rank, yet every layer computes them at full size. CoLA builds the low rank into the architecture: each linear layer becomes a small auto-encoder. Models come out 2× smaller and training takes half the compute, at full-rank perplexity.

Abstract

The full-size MLPs and the projection layers in attention introduce tremendous model sizes of large language models (LLMs), consuming extensive computational resources in pre-training. We empirically observe that the activations of pre-trained LLMs exhibit low-rank property. Motivated by such observations, we propose CoLA and its memory-efficient implementation, CoLA-M, to replace these full-size layers with compute-efficient auto-encoders that naturally enforce low-rank activations throughout training. This fundamental architectural change eliminates the activation redundancy and significantly boosts model capacity and training efficiency. Experiments on LLaMA models with 60 million to 7 billion parameters show that CoLA reduces the computing cost by 2× and improves training throughput by 1.86× while maintaining full-rank level performance. CoLA-M further squeezes memory cost without sacrificing throughput, offering a pre-training approach with collectively superior parameter, computing, and memory efficiency. The LLMs produced are also 2× smaller, enabling faster inference with lower memory cost on resource-constrained platforms.

Low-rank activation by construction

In a pre-trained GPT-2, the singular values of the MLP activations fall off quickly: in every block, the rank that keeps 95% of the spectral energy is a small fraction of the full dimension. Full-size layers spend most of their compute producing that redundancy.

CoLA replaces each linear layer with h = B σ(Ax), where r is well below both input and output sizes. The nonlinearity sits between the two factors, so the activation itself stays low-rank. That differs from factorizing W into BA, which compresses parameters but keeps a full-size activation. All MLP layers and all attention projections become CoLA layers; attention itself, residual connections and the gated product stay as they are.

With the default rank r = d/4, a decoder layer needs about half the compute of full-rank training, and CoLA saves compute for any r below about 0.62d. The other efficient pre-training methods each cover only part of the picture:

What the theory says

  1. Never worse than plain low rank. A CoLA layer approximates a layer at least as well as a standard low-rank layer of the same rank. Proposition 3.1
  2. Strictly better under data-dependent conditions. The nonlinearity in the middle lets it beat the standard low-rank layer. Theorem 3.3
  3. Benefits from low effective rank. Its error bound depends on the output's effective rank, which is small in practice. Theorem 3.4

CoLA-M: save only the small activations

At large batch sizes, activations can take most of the memory. Plain gradient checkpointing keeps each block's input and recomputes the whole block in the backward pass, almost a full forward pass. CoLA-M keeps the low-rank activations instead, and recomputes only the up-projections and self-attention, about half of a CoLA forward pass.

At a similar memory saving on LLaMA-1B, that is 4.6× less recomputation than plain checkpointing. CoLA-M needs a quarter of full-rank training's memory at 1B and a third at 7B, and it still trains faster.

Results

LLaMA models from 60M to 7B parameters pre-trained on C4 with no data repetition, following the exact setup of GaLore and SLTrain, plus BERT-Large and an over-trained LLaMA-350M.

Smallest model, full-rank perplexity

At every size CoLA has the fewest parameters and the least memory, and its perplexity is on par with full-rank training: 15.52 against 15.56 at 1B.

LLaMA-7B with a third of the memory

At 150k steps, CoLA-M reaches a perplexity of 12.73 with 26.8 GB of memory. 8-bit Adam and 8-bit GaLore stay around 14.6 while using 65 to 73 GB.

A larger rank beats full-rank training

Raising the rank to 0.7× of the full-rank compute makes CoLA better than full-rank training at all three sizes. Shrinking a full-rank model to CoLA's default budget instead costs a lot of perplexity.

Beyond compute-optimal

Trained 8× past the compute-optimal budget (51B tokens), LLaMA-350M with CoLA reaches 13.96 against 14.47 for full rank. A CoLA version of BERT-Large (350M) pre-trained on 85B tokens ends at a lower loss (1.257 against 1.263), and after fine-tuning it averages 83.5 on GLUE against 82.7, ahead or level on 7 of 8 tasks.

Faster to train and to serve

CoLA and CoLA-M are the only methods here that train faster than the full-rank model. At inference, CoLA is up to 1.64× faster and uses up to 1.67× less memory.

Limitations and follow-up work

  • Distributed training. Scaling CoLA further needs proper model parallelism, and porting it to frameworks such as Megatron-LM and DeepSpeed is not trivial.
  • Industrial scale. The industrial-setting check stops at BERT-size models. Decoder-only models with billions of parameters trained on trillions of tokens are still to be tested, and we are open to collaborating on that.
  • Mixture-of-experts. The paper covers dense models only. Extending CoLA to MoE architectures is left for future work.
  • Follow-up work. LaX (NeurIPS 2025), LOST and CR-Net build on CoLA and improve it further.
